The verification layer

One rule: nobody trusts their own work. A cheap worker tries the job. A separate checker decides if the result is actually good. Only then is it accepted.

How it works

Step 1 — the cheap worker goes first

A small, fast, cheap AI model tries the task. It costs almost nothing. Most of the time it gets it right.

Step 2 — an independent checker judges

A different model — not the worker, not shown the worker's reasoning — looks at the result like a stranger: "Is this correct? Would this actually work?" Because it didn't do the work, it doesn't defend it.

Step 3 — pass or fail

Pass → accepted. You paid the cheap price.
Fail → the task goes to a big, expensive model. You only pay that price when the cheap try truly failed.

The specifics

  • Memory loop around it — four stages per task: execute (worker runs), capture (episode saved), consolidate (lessons extracted into an episode store), inject (lessons re-enter context on the next task). The system's own history becomes its material.
  • Three parts in the gate — a competence estimator (per task-pattern: is the cheap tier likely admissible here?), the cheap attempt, and the independent verifier that adjudicates. The large tier runs only on failed verification.
  • How the estimator estimates — it counts, it doesn't predict. For a new task it finds past episodes of similar tasks where the cheap model actually attempted, and its score is simply the share the verifier accepted: ŝ = accepts / n. Neighborhoods with fewer than m₀ attempts are padded with failures: ŝ = accepts / max(n, m₀). So one real attempt that passed reads 1/3 = 33%, never 100% — a single fluke can't open the gate. As real attempts accumulate they replace the padding, and the score climbs only on deserved data. Missing evidence counts as failure; nothing is faked. (Plus a small probe rate: even a thin neighborhood occasionally gets a cheap try, so no pattern is exiled forever.)
  • Break-even rule — the gate saves money only when the cheap tier's acceptance probability q beats the cost ratio: q > ccheap / clarge. Below that line, skip the gate and always use the large tier.
  • Safety bound — even the worst gate degrades quality only in proportion to the verifier's false-accept rate. The verifier is the one component that can silently hurt you, so it's held to the strict standard.
  • The failure mode to know about — an audit of 1,225 real episodes found the gate fired 0 times in 153 decisions: attempts that never happen are never recorded, so the estimator can never learn why (cold-start non-identifiability). Fix: force early cheap attempts so the estimator gets data.

Why the name "Archimedes"

Archimedes said: give me a place to stand and I will move the world. The checker is that place to stand — a fixed point outside the work. A model grading its own homework always gives itself the benefit of the doubt. The verification layer removes that.

Why it saves money

Cheap worker right 80% of the time at 20× lower cost → 8 of 10 tasks cost almost nothing, 2 pay full price. Same quality, much smaller bill.

One sentence: let the cheap model try, let an independent model judge, and only pay the expensive model when the judge says no.
